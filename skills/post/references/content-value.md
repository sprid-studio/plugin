# Choose what the post gives the viewer

Use before drafting, extending a series or diagnosing repetitive AI-assisted
content. Read the account's voice and accepted brief first. These are editorial
hypotheses to test for this audience, not a universal ranking formula.

## Start with material, then choose the format

Write a compact brief in the existing post notes or campaign plan:
the viewer's situation, the promised payoff, the actual material delivering it,
and the desired response. The payoff can be useful information, recognition or
entertainment. An app demonstration is appropriate when the task calls for one;
do not turn every story into a product pitch.

Look for material the account can contribute specifically: a real product result,
an observed detail, an interpretation with evidence, or a distinctive scene.
Ask what would remain useful or enjoyable if the logo and call to action vanished.
This question helps choose the substance; it is not an automatic rejection gate.

| Material available | A concrete treatment | Check before claiming it |
|---|---|---|
| A working product flow | Show the starting problem and the actual result, with enough of the action to understand the connection. | Record the real flow; keep mockups separate from shipped capability. |
| A place, object or source image | Point out one visible detail, then explain what it changes about how the viewer sees the subject. | Verify the detail and its source; distinguish documentary media from reconstructions. |
| A decision with tradeoffs | Compare examples using the same stated criteria and show when each fits. | Verify both sides; do not invent a test or a customer's result. |
| An audience question or recurring situation | Answer with a worked example, or dramatize the situation in the account's voice. | Separate a sourced quote from rewritten dialogue; never present invented testimony as real. |

AI can help research, edit, translate, illustrate and produce variants. Verify its
factual contributions. A template or generated visual does not establish that the
post is interchangeable; examine the delivered idea. Human footage alone does
not establish value either. Follow the destination's current disclosure rules.

## Make the series progress

Keep a recognizable promise or format while giving each episode a new example,
finding, question or narrative turn. Describe that difference before drafting a
batch. A caption paraphrase alone is not a new episode. Adaptations for another
language or audience may reuse substance when that is the intended task.

Use real questions from comments, support and research for subsequent episodes.
Read the linked source before quoting; preserve consent and context for personal
stories. Do not invent questions to imply an audience response that never happened.

For search-oriented posts, name the actual subject and answer the relevant query
in the viewer's language. Keep titles and captions consistent with the content;
do not add unrelated trending terms. A useful worked example may also belong on
an accessible product page: use the [AI discovery method](../../seo-pages/references/ai-discovery.md)
there. A social post alone is not evidence that assistants will retrieve it.

## Test the contribution, not just the packaging

Compare the proposed treatment with the account's existing approach on comparable
topics, destinations and post ages. Separate paid distribution and meaningful
changes in length, audience or offer. Organic comparisons suggest hypotheses;
they do not isolate every cause. Keep the account's requested volume and scope.

Select the outcome before publishing: for example, a useful audience response
for a recognition post, or attributable activation for an acquisition post.
Use available watch and engagement metrics to diagnose the result, with sample
sizes and denominators. Missing retention or conversion data stays missing.
Views alone do not establish customer acquisition or audience trust.

If a series weakens, inspect topic changes, publishing failures and distribution
before calling it audience fatigue. Repeat a promising treatment across comparable
examples before generalizing. A weak result on one post does not prove that AI,
explainers or carousels have stopped working. If the proposed contribution fails
to improve the declared outcome across comparable observations, revise it.

## What platform statements support

Sources checked 2026-10-31. Re-read the relevant source before acting on policy.
Keep the platform and the kind of rule attached to every claim:

- **Instagram recommendations:** Meta's [original-content announcement](https://about.fb.com/ltam/news/2024/05/ayudando-a-los-creadores-a-encontrar-nuevas-audiencias/)
  describes replacing matching reposts with the original in recommendations, with
  exceptions for substantial transformations. This does not promise reach for a
  new post or prohibit AI-assisted production.
- **Facebook Feed and Reels:** Meta's [March 2026 guidance](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)
  deprioritizes copied content and minor alterations; meaningful new information
  or analysis can qualify as original. Do not apply its detailed criteria to
  Instagram as if the platforms share one rule.
- **TikTok:** its [AI-content guidance](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content)
  says enabling the AI label does not affect distribution when content complies
  with its guidelines. Its [recommendation explanation](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)
  describes viewer interactions and search relevance. Neither establishes an
  account's optimal cadence, slide count or expected reach.
- **YouTube monetization:** [channel policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
  exclude repetitive or mass-produced content from monetization while allowing
  recurring formats with materially varied substance. This is a Partner Program
  rule, not evidence of an automatic recommendation penalty for every AI video.

The recommendation here is to build reusable formats around worthwhile material.
These statements do not establish a blanket AI-content penalty or forecast when
audiences will tire of a format; those remain questions for observed behavior.
