# Why a post travels

**What this guide does:** Explains what Instagram and TikTok actually reward,
and turns each mechanic into a rule you can build a post against. Read it before
deciding a format, a slide count or a caption length, and when a post that
looked good got no reach.

Whether a post is *good* and whether it *travels* are two different questions.
This one is about the second. Every rule here exists because of a specific
mechanic, and if the mechanic changes the rule goes with it, so each one carries
its reason.

**Verified against published sources 2026-08-04.** Platform mechanics decay.
Treat anything here as a claim about a moving system, re-check it quarterly, and
prefer your own numbers the moment you have them.

## Instagram

**The three named ranking signals are watch time, sends per reach, and likes per
reach.** Sends are the heaviest, reported at roughly three to five times the
weight of a like. Sends per reach is specifically the signal that reaches people
who do not follow you, which is the only reach a new account can grow on.

**Feed, Reels, Stories and Explore rank separately** and weight those signals
differently. Feed leans on how close you already are to the viewer; Explore on
engagement velocity and interest match. A post that does well with your existing
followers is not automatically a post that travels.

**Why carousels, specifically:**

- Every swipe is engagement, and dwell time accumulates across the slides.
  Carousels are reported at two to three times the reach of a single image for
  the same content.
- **The platform re-serves a carousel to people who did not swipe, starting from
  the second slide.** This is the most actionable mechanic available: slide two
  gets an independent second chance to be someone's first impression.
- Carousels out-save single images by a wide margin, and saves compound, because
  saved posts get resurfaced.
- Completion - how many people reach the last slide - is what pushes a post out
  of your followers and into Explore.

**Hashtags do not drive distribution.** The platform's own position is that they
categorise rather than distribute. Discovery comes from the words in your
caption: captions, alt text, bios and on-screen text are indexed and served into
in-app search. Keyword-rich captions have been measured at around 30% more reach
than hashtag-heavy ones. Keep three to five hashtags as labels and spend the
effort on the prose.

## TikTok, photo mode

- Ranking is swipe-through rate, dwell time and reverse swipes, with completion
  rate as the primary signal.
- Distribution starts with a **small test batch of roughly 200-500 viewers**,
  mostly followers and people who engage with adjacent content. What that batch
  does decides everything afterwards. This is why the first hour matters, and
  why a weak second slide can end the post’s reach.
- Saves are weighted and photo posts save well. A carousel with fewer views but
  a high save-and-comment share is doing better than a higher-view post that
  bounces on slide one.
- Interest-based distribution means an outlier is possible from your first post.
  TikTok is spikier; Instagram grinds.

## What follows for how you build a post

| Rule | Because |
|---|---|
| **Slide 2 is a second hook.** It delivers on slide 1 *and* opens a new thread, and it has to work cold | the platform re-serves from slide 2; TikTok's test batch dies there |
| **Seven slides for a narrative format** (hook, five body, closer) | seven to ten is the reported dwell-time sweet spot; under five reads as a short post, over ten causes mid-carousel fatigue |
| **No slide may be skippable.** If a body slide can be removed without breaking the post, you wrote a list, and people can stop anywhere in a list | completion is the ranking signal, and a list lets people stop anywhere |
| **The peak lands in the last third** | a middle peak makes the tail a letdown, and the tail is where completion is won |
| **Design for sends.** At least one slide should make someone think of a specific person | sends per reach is the non-follower signal, worth several likes |
| **The send prompt lives in the caption**, naming one kind of person, never "share if you relate" | it belongs where sends are earned, and it keeps the last slide screenshottable |
| **At least two slides hand over something usable** | recognition earns likes; recognition plus something doable earns saves, and saves compound |
| **Captions carry the audience's own search phrases, in prose** | captions are indexed; hashtags are not distribution |
| **Caption length 400-600 characters on Instagram, 150-300 on TikTok**, first line an independent hook under 125 characters | that is where the "more" cut falls; past roughly 300 characters TikTok needs a tap and reach drops |
| **Three to five hashtags, never the generic feed tag** | labels, not reach |
| **One canvas at 4:5, content clear of the top and bottom edges** | survives the 3:4 grid crop, letterboxes acceptably on TikTok |

## Cadence, and the first hour

**Three to five posts a week, sustained.** Three a week for twelve weeks beats
seven a week for three. The failure mode is not low quality, it is stopping - and
the documented version of it is 34 drafts and 3 published posts.

**The first hour is the test batch.** Being there to answer early comments is the
cheapest intervention available on either platform; a comment answered in the
first hour is worth more than the same reply a day later.

**Post at a fixed time** your audience is awake for, set once on the account's
posting schedule rather than decided per post.

**Expect silence for two months.** A new account with no face takes three to six
months to reach a thousand followers on Instagram. Distribution is a power law:
a few posts carry most of the reach. Judging before 90 days is judging noise.

## When something works, fan it out

When a post breaks out, the work starts: more like it, and the best one promoted.

1. Make five to ten variants of the winner with the structure fixed and **exactly
   one variable changed** - the hook phrasing, the register, the opening image.
2. One variable per variant, or the result teaches nothing.
3. Log which variant won *and* which source line its hook came from. That tells
   you which well to keep digging.
4. A losing variant tells you something. Stop it and write down what.

## What is worth not believing

Everything above is published guidance and platform statements, not our
measurements. Before you treat any of it as settled for **your** audience, these
are the clean one-variable tests: caption length judged on saves and sends per
reach; a quiet text card against a photo behind text; a native 9:16 crop against
a letterboxed 4:5; whether a screenshot of your app costs reach or buys installs.

Log the real numbers per post at seven days - views, completion, saves, sends,
comments, profile taps. The moment you have your own numbers they outrank every
source below.
