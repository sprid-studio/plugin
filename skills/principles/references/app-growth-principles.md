# App growth decision checks

These are working hypotheses and measurement methods. They carry no inherited
verification status or performance benchmark. Validate them against the customer's
product, audience and business model before recommending a change.

## Creative and messaging

- **Learn from customer language.** Read relevant reviews, interviews, support
  conversations and public discussions. Distinguish first-hand experience from
  advice. Preserve sources and separate evidence from drafted copy.
- **Match the voice to the account.** Brand, founder, educational and entertainment
  accounts can use different registers. Derive perspective, imagery, punctuation
  and calls to action from the customer's brief and existing copy.
- **Test repeatable formats.** Compare variants with a stated hypothesis and a
  relevant outcome. A successful post can justify more variants; an untested
  format can justify an experiment. Neither guarantees repeatable performance.
- **Make the destination clear.** When the goal is an install or signup, connect
  the creative's promise to the actual product and landing experience. Choose
  when and how to mention the app according to the format and campaign objective.

## Acquisition and distribution

- **Choose channels by audience and evidence.** Inspect where relevant users
  already search, discuss and discover products. Prioritize a channel when focus
  helps learning; parallel channels may serve distinct audiences or objectives.
  Use the customer's constraints rather than a fixed calendar prohibition.
- **Evaluate paid acquisition with unit economics.** Compare acquisition cost
  with contribution value over a stated horizon, accounting for refunds, fees,
  retention and attribution uncertainty. Revenue per install suits some mobile
  apps; qualified leads or paid accounts may suit other products. When data is
  missing, propose an explicitly bounded measurement experiment. Drafting does
  not authorize spend; launching requires the user's approval.
- **Treat engagement as an intermediate outcome.** Views, saves and shares can
  inform creative decisions. Use attributable visits, activation, purchases or
  another declared product outcome for business decisions. State attribution gaps.
- **Keep distribution state visible.** Distinguish drafts, approvals, scheduled
  rows and confirmed publishes. Diagnose the step that prevents distribution
  before recommending more inventory, without blocking requested creative work.

## Product value and pricing

- **Validate activation and retention definitions.** Trace successful product use
  to emitting code and durable records. Separate guided exercises from independent
  use. Compare cohorts with matching eligibility and sufficient follow-up.
- **Test pricing against customer value.** A competitor's price is context, not a
  target. Evaluate conversion, contribution and renewal together. Keep platform,
  market and offer differences visible.
- **Choose trial design from observed behavior.** Ask whether users experience
  value before the trial ends and whether they subsequently retain. Trial, paid
  introduction and direct payment are testable options; none is a universal default.
- **Check constraints before scaling.** Identify the measured failure in the
  acquisition-to-value path. Name the observation that would change the proposed
  priority. A small sample or instrumentation gap is a limit on the conclusion.

## Evidence to attach

For each recommendation, record the question, source, date range, cohort definition,
comparison and limitation. Label it observed, inferred or untested in this product.
Use current primary sources for external benchmarks and platform requirements.
Keep customer-specific metrics and investigation paths in the customer's repo;
never copy them into this shared reference.
