# Programmatic content and SEO checks

Use these methods for directories, catalogues and other sites with repeated page
shapes. They are questions to test on the customer's data. Timing, position and
sample thresholds must come from that site; no private case-study metrics apply.

## Distribution and comparisons

1. Report the median, sample size and share of traffic held by the leading page
   beside each category mean. Recompute without that page before calling a category
   strong. Concentration can make a category ranking describe a single item.
2. Cohort pages by publication date and indexing state. Measure when performance
   stabilizes before judging a new batch. Choose a review window from that curve.
3. Compare clicks, impressions, CTR and position within relevant markets. High
   impression volume alone does not establish that adding similar pages will help.
4. Split pooled rankings by market, language, page template and relevant audience.
   Investigate reversals before recommending a shared content strategy.
5. Compare completeness bands, including missing descriptions and images. Separate
   basic coverage from added depth and check whether age or demand explains the gap.
6. Count distinct users as well as events for product-based rankings. Show sparse
   segments as uncertain and test how rankings change when repeat users are removed.

## Coverage and selection bias

7. Exclude verified internal identities and separate onboarding-generated actions
   from independent use. Derive the exclusion from the actual flow; a fixed elapsed
   time can discard legitimate activity and still retain tutorial events.
8. Inspect query-plus-page pairs before mining search for missing inventory. Search
   reports observe demand for pages already surfaced and may omit rare queries;
   they cannot establish all unmet demand.
9. Audit categories below publishing thresholds separately. A bucket with no page
   cannot earn search impressions. Look for independent demand evidence there.
10. Join unfulfilled searches or empty navigation results to available inventory.
    Rank by distinct people and repeat demand, keeping uncertain geography or
    incomplete telemetry visible. Event volume alone can describe one session.

## Alternative explanations

11. Compare existing page templates and facets before investing in another one.
    Separate search CTR from product conversion, and control for age and position.
12. Separate arrival from engagement. Dwell time needs its sample and measurement
    definition; longer pages can change the metric without adding customer value.
13. Test whether popularity or competition explains content performance. Use an
    independent proxy when available; an importer's saved metadata may reflect
    pipeline coverage rather than demand. Famous and obscure items may serve
    different acquisition and retention goals.
14. Stratify a market comparison by content mix and competition before attributing
    its advantage to geography or language. Keep both explanations if both survive.

## Build the customer's join

Read Search Console page data and, when needed, query-plus-page data for an explicit
window. Join it to the customer's content inventory and authorized product analytics.
Retain publication date, market, template and the fields needed for the hypothesis.

Validate canonical URLs and aliases before joining. Report unmatched and ambiguous
URLs, their share of traffic, and whether missingness differs across segments. Do
not assume missing rows are uniformly distributed or multiply results to fill gaps.

For every proposed action, state the supporting observation, a competing explanation
and the observation that would reverse the conclusion. Store app-specific queries
and results in the customer's existing investigation index, not in this plugin.
