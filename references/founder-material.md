# Founder material

Pages and posts written only from the repository and public sources read like
everyone else's. What makes them worth ranking or sharing is what only the
person behind the product knows: a case, a number, a decision. Collect it before
drafting, and never invent it.

## Ask before drafting

Ask one question at a time, in the user's language, and stop as soon as one
answer gives a concrete anchor. Skip any that do not fit the topic.

1. **Story.** The most recent time the product made a difference for someone,
   in this topic. What happened, specifically?
2. **Number.** Anything measurable from that case a reader could check.
3. **What you don't do.** A choice that sets the product apart from the obvious
   option, and why you made it.
4. **Opinion.** What most people in this category get wrong.
5. **Proof.** A screenshot, measurement or quote that may be published.

An anchor is something checkable and particular: a case with a detail, a
decision someone can disagree with, an opinion with an example, or a number
with its source. Read existing material first (founder
notes, earlier reports, accepted guidance) and ask only for what is missing.

## When there is no anchor

Say so and do not draft. Offer the choices instead: a nearby topic where the user
does have material, the work that would produce it (a test, a customer
conversation, a query against the product's own data), or a plain version that
is unlikely to stand out. Never supply a customer, number or quote yourself.

## Keep it

Save the answers verbatim as guidance for the app, with the date and the topic
they answered. Opinions stay in the founder's words; polishing them into
marketing copy removes what made them useful. Record what the offer may and may
not promise, and attempts that did not work, beside them. Later drafts read this
material before asking again.
